## 2. Research Identity

### 2.1 Core Theme

The research investigates how soil microbiome composition, soil physicochemical properties, climate variables, and orchard metadata can be integrated using Hybrid AI and Explainable AI to predict mango yield and disease risk in the Malihabad mango belt of Uttar Pradesh.

## 3. Problem Statement

Mango productivity is strongly affected by soil fertility, microbial diversity, pathogenic load, climate stress, and orchard management practices. These factors interact in nonlinear and high-dimensional ways. Conventional soil testing and manual interpretation are often insufficient to predict yield loss, disease risk, or soil-health decline with enough precision.

The research problem is to develop an interpretable AI-based framework that can connect soil microbiome patterns and soil/climate indicators with mango yield and crop-health outcomes, especially for Malihabad's mango-growing ecosystem.

## 4. Research Gap

The proposal and dataset summary identify the following gaps:

1. Mango-specific microbiome prediction models are limited.
2. Location-specific soil microbiome datasets for Malihabad mango orchards are not readily available.
3. Existing AI applications in agriculture often lack explainability.
4. Microbiome studies often remain descriptive and do not translate into farmer-facing decision tools.
5. There is limited integration of soil chemistry, microbial taxa, functional microbial groups, climate data, orchard metadata, and agronomic targets in a single predictive framework.
6. Field validation is still required because the present CSV is synthetic.

## 5. Overall Aim

To design, develop, evaluate, and explain a Hybrid AI framework that uses soil microbiome, soil physicochemical, climate, and orchard metadata to predict mango yield, disease risk, and nutrient availability for sustainable mango cultivation in Malihabad, U.P.

## 6. Research Objectives

### 6.1 Scientific Objectives

1. Characterize key soil microbiome indicators relevant to mango orchard health.
2. Integrate microbial, soil, climate, and agronomic variables into a unified dataset.
3. Identify the most influential predictors of mango yield and disease risk.
4. Model nonlinear relationships between soil microbiome composition and crop outcomes.
5. Use XAI methods to interpret model predictions at global and individual-orchard levels.

### 6.2 Technical Objectives

1. Build a clean machine-learning-ready dataset.
2. Create engineered features such as `Soil_Health_Index`, `Microbial_Richness_Score`, `Nutrient_Balance_Ratio`, and `Pathogen_Load_Index`.
3. Train regression models for `Mango_Yield`.
4. Train classification models for `Disease_Risk` and `Nutrient_Availability`.
5. Compare machine learning, deep learning, and hybrid rule-based approaches.
6. Generate SHAP and LIME explanations for model decisions.
7. Develop a decision support system prototype for prediction and recommendations.

### 6.3 Practical Objectives

1. Help farmers and agronomists identify soil-health risks early.
2. Support microbial and nutrient management decisions.
3. Reduce unnecessary chemical input by promoting targeted interventions.
4. Improve mango yield stability and orchard sustainability.
5. Provide an interpretable digital tool for soil-health decision support.

## 7. Research Questions

1. Which soil physicochemical parameters are most strongly associated with mango yield?
2. Which microbiome diversity indices and taxa are most predictive of soil health?
3. How strongly does pathogen load influence mango disease risk?
4. Can engineered soil-health and microbial-richness indicators improve prediction accuracy?
5. Do tree-based machine learning models outperform deep learning models on this structured tabular dataset?
6. Can explainability methods translate AI predictions into agronomically meaningful recommendations?
7. Does a model trained on synthetic or multi-village data generalize to a geographically held-out village such as Rahimabad?
8. What real-world sampling design is needed to validate the synthetic-data findings?

## 8. Working Hypotheses

1. Higher soil health and nutrient balance are positively associated with mango yield.
2. Higher pathogen load, especially linked with `Fusarium`, increases disease risk.
3. Tree-based ensemble models such as Random Forest and Gradient Boosting are well suited for this structured tabular dataset.
4. XAI methods can identify interpretable agronomic drivers such as pH, EC, potassium availability, pathogen load, and microbial richness.
5. A decision support system can make the model outputs useful for farmers, researchers, and extension workers.

## 18. Concise Research Story

This research begins with the agricultural challenge of understanding mango orchard soil health in Malihabad. It proposes that soil microbiome composition, nutrient balance, pathogen pressure, and climate variables can be combined into a predictive AI framework. A synthetic dataset with 20,000 samples and 63 variables has been created to prototype the pipeline. The reported modeling results suggest that Gradient Boosting and Random Forest are strong candidates for yield and disease-risk prediction, while SHAP and LIME can explain the agronomic meaning of the predictions. The next essential step is to make the pipeline reproducible with code and then validate it using real soil microbiome samples collected from mango orchards.
