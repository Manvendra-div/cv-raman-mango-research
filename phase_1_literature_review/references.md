# Phase 1 References

This source list supports the Phase 1 literature matrix, gap statement, objectives, and technical workflow. It favors official sources, peer-reviewed research, and widely used method papers.

## Local and Crop Context

**R1. APEDA. Mango product page.**  
Official export and production context for Indian mango, including the 2023-24 note that Uttar Pradesh ranked first in mango production.  
URL: https://apeda.gov.in/apedawebsite/SubHead_Products/Mango.htm

**R2. Intellectual Property India. Mango Malihabadi Dusseheri, GI Application 125.**  
Official GI record showing registration status, applicant, certificate date, and validity for Mango Malihabadi Dusseheri.  
URL: https://search.ipindia.gov.in/GIRPublicSearch/Application/Details/125

## Mango Microbiome and Mango Disease Ecology

**R3. Kumar, G., Suman, A., Lal, S., Ram, R. A., Bhatt, P., Pandey, G., Chaudhary, P., et al. (2021). Bacterial structure and dynamics in mango orchards after long term organic and conventional treatments under subtropical ecosystem. Scientific Reports, 11, 20554.**  
Shows that long-term organic and conventional mango orchard management changes rhizosphere bacterial structure and soil biological activity in Dashehari mango systems.  
URL: https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-021-00112-0  
DOI: https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-021-00112-0

**R4. Liew, E. C. Y., Laurence, M. H., Pearce, C. A., Shivas, R. G., Johnson, G. I., Tan, Y. P., Edwards, J., Perry, S., Cooke, A. W., & Summerell, B. A. (2016). Review of Fusarium species isolated in association with mango malformation in Australia. Australasian Plant Pathology, 45, 547-559.**  
Supports the disease-ecology relevance of Fusarium-linked mango malformation.  
URL: https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s13313-016-0454-z  
DOI: https://doi.org/10.1007/s13313-016-0454-z

**R5. Mango anthracnose disease: the current situation and direction for future research.**  
Review of mango anthracnose and the role of Colletotrichum complexes in mango disease losses.  
URL: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10484599/

## Soil Microbiome, Soil Health, and Plant Health

**R6. Berendsen, R. L., Pieterse, C. M. J., & Bakker, P. A. H. M. (2012). The rhizosphere microbiome and plant health. Trends in Plant Science, 17, 478-486.**  
Foundational review connecting rhizosphere microbial communities with plant health and disease suppression.  
URL: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22564542/  
DOI: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tplants.2012.04.001

**R7. Mendes, R., Kruijt, M., de Bruijn, I., Dekkers, E., van der Voort, M., Schneider, J. H. M., et al. (2011). Deciphering the rhizosphere microbiome for disease-suppressive bacteria. Science, 332, 1097-1100.**  
Primary evidence that disease-suppressive soils can be linked to microbial community signatures.  
URL: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21551032/  
DOI: https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1203980

**R8. Trivedi, P., Leach, J. E., Tringe, S. G., Sa, T., & Singh, B. K. (2020). Plant-microbiome interactions: from community assembly to plant health. Nature Reviews Microbiology, 18, 607-621.**  
Review connecting plant microbiome assembly, nutrient acquisition, stress response, and plant health.  
URL: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32788714/  
DOI: https://doi.org/10.1038/s41579-020-0412-1

**R9. Fierer, N. (2017). Embracing the unknown: disentangling the complexities of the soil microbiome. Nature Reviews Microbiology, 15, 579-590.**  
Frames soil microbiomes as high-dimensional, complex ecological systems requiring careful interpretation.  
URL: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28824177/  
DOI: https://doi.org/10.1038/nrmicro.2017.87

**R10. A review on effective soil health bio-indicators for ecosystem restoration and sustainability.**  
Supports the need to combine biological, chemical, and physical indicators in soil-health assessment.  
URL: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9428492/

## AI, ML, and Microbiome Prediction

Broad review of machine learning use in agricultural production systems.  
URL: https://www.mdpi.com/1424-8220/18/8/2674  
DOI: https://doi.org/10.3390/s18082674

**R12. Benos, L., Tagarakis, A. C., Dolias, G., Berruto, R., Kateris, D., & Bochtis, D. (2021). Machine Learning in Agriculture: A Comprehensive Updated Review. Sensors, 21, 3758.**  
Updated PRISMA-style review covering ML in crop, water, soil, and livestock management.  
URL: https://www.mdpi.com/1424-8220/21/11/3758  
DOI: https://doi.org/10.3390/s21113758

**R13. van Klompenburg, T., Kassahun, A., & Catal, C. (2020). Crop yield prediction using machine learning: A systematic literature review. Computers and Electronics in Agriculture, 177, 105709.**  
Supports model-comparison planning for crop-yield prediction.  
DOI: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.compag.2020.105709

**R14. Pace, R., Schiano Di Cola, V., Monti, M. M., Affinito, A., Cuomo, S., Loreto, F., et al. (2024). Artificial intelligence in soil microbiome analysis: a potential application in predicting and enhancing soil health - a review. Discover Applied Sciences.**  
Directly supports AI use for soil microbiome analysis and soil-health prediction.  
URL: https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s42452-024-06381-4  
DOI: https://doi.org/10.1007/s42452-024-06381-4

**R15. Wilhelm, R. C., van Es, H. M., & Buckley, D. H. (2022). Predicting measures of soil health using the microbiome and supervised machine learning. Soil Biology and Biochemistry, 164, 108472.**  
Evidence that supervised ML can connect microbiome data to soil-health measures.  
DOI: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.soilbio.2021.108472

## Explainable AI and Decision Support

**R17. Ribeiro, M. T., Singh, S., & Guestrin, C. (2016). Why Should I Trust You? Explaining the Predictions of Any Classifier. KDD.**

DOI: https://doi.org/10.1145/2939672.2939778

**R19. Zhai, Z., Martinez Ortega, J. F., Beltran, V., & Lucas Martinez, N. (2020). Decision support systems for agriculture 4.0: Survey and challenges. Computers and Electronics in Agriculture, 170, 105256.**

URL: https://oa.upm.es/63393/  
DOI: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.compag.2020.105256
